# Trippy Daydream hero art — short design

**Why:** the client read the Daydream shopfront/interior art as a candy shop. Babajee's art and brand identity must feel psychedelic and trippy while staying in the Daydream rendering style.

**Uses:** notification emails, onboarding, category header cards, marketing, more later.

**Goals:** one visual family across every slot; trippy, not sweet; readable at small sizes (emails, cards).
**Non-goals:** product art (never generated, see no-product-art memory); redrawing the logo.
**Anti-goals:** bubblegum pink as the dominant colour, cute sleepy faces, pastel softness: anything that reads "sweets".

## Keep from Daydream
Ink outlines, riso halftone dots, slightly misregistered flat colour, hand-printed paper grain, stars and ringed planets.

## Change
- Ground: deep indigo / near-black instead of cream (cream only as paper grain and highlights).
- Palette: hot magenta, acid green, tangerine, electric cyan, mustard. Magenta is an accent, never the field.
- Motifs: op-art spiral suns, concentric ripple bands, warped/melting hills, smoke that curls into paisley, an all-seeing eye. At most one face per piece, cosmic and half-lidded, never cute.
- No text, no logo, no products in the art (the site and emails add those).

## Approaches
- **A. Master artwork first, derive the rest (chosen).** One wide key art fixes the look; Nefer approves it; every slot is generated with it as the style reference. Consistent; hinges on one image.
- B. Motif kit composed in code. Cheapest per slot, but clip-art feel. Used later, cut from the approved master, for small repeated slots.
- C. One prompt per slot. Fast, but drifts: likely how Daydream became candy.

## Tool and process
ChatGPT image generation in Nefer's Chrome, on the device he names only (he is on a friend's account). One generation at a time; Nefer approves each before the next. Generated files are parked, never deleted.

## Order
1. Master key art, 16:9.
2. Style sheet (exact hexes + do/don't) written from the approved master.
3. Onboarding band (phone, ~4:3), email header (3:1), category cards (square, one per category), marketing (1:1, 4:5, 9:16).
